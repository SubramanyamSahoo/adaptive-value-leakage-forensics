# Figure Captions

## Figure 1 — Behavioral value leakage
Directional leakage is P(final above threshold | above-good) minus P(final above threshold | below-good). Error bars are 95% nonparametric bootstrap intervals.

## Figure 2 — Component-level distortion
Above-good minus below-good log-median shifts for giraffe population and spots per giraffe. The relevant intervention fixes spots per giraffe at the locked neutral median of 792.5.

## Figure 3 — Boundary tracking
With spots per giraffe fixed at 792.5, low/high final-answer thresholds induce low/high population boundaries from neutral population quantiles. Points show median extracted population estimates with 95% bootstrap intervals.

## Figure 4 — Preference-directed population crossing
Difference between above-good and below-good in the probability that chosen population exceeds each manipulated boundary.

## Figure 5 — Final-answer crossing
Difference between above-good and below-good in the probability of a final estimate above each manipulated threshold.

## Main interpretation
The initially identified high-slack component is preference-sensitive, and fixing it removes its treatment-dependent distortion. Behavioral leakage nevertheless survives. A follow-up boundary manipulation produces suggestive positive population-boundary tracking, but preference-directed crossing remains statistically uncertain at the available sample size.
